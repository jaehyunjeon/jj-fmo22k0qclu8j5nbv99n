Preconditioning Dynamics of Resonant Inverse Design

Ongoing research

Stanford University · Fan Group · 2026

Figure coming soon

Abstract

High-Q resonant inverse design is notoriously ill-conditioned. Using temporal coupled-mode theory, I reduce the optimization to a two-dimensional physical state that separates resonance detuning from useful enhancement, and show that the familiar O(Q²) stiffness is not intrinsic to the objective but is introduced by the design parameterization. The reduced dynamics have an exact phase structure, which explains why reparameterization, not learning-rate tuning, is the right lever.

Motivation

Optimizing high-Q resonators by gradient descent is known to be difficult: as Q grows, the landscape becomes stiff and step sizes must shrink. This is usually treated as an unavoidable property of resonant objectives. I asked where this ill-conditioning actually comes from, and what a principled remedy would look like.

Approach

  • Rewrote a field-intensity objective with temporal coupled-mode theory in a two-dimensional physical state: resonance detuning and useful resonant enhancement.
  • Represented the design parameterization through its Jacobian-induced kernel K = JJT and projected it onto this reduced state, collapsing a high-dimensional optimization step into a closed-form reduced map.
  • Derived the full phase structure of the reduced dynamics analytically.

Key findings

  • The O(Q²) stiffness is not intrinsic to the resonant objective; it appears when the physical dynamics are pulled back through the parameterization.
  • Three exact regimes: clean tracking, transient stall, and runaway detuning. The objective can keep increasing even in the runaway regime, so objective ascent alone does not certify resonance tracking.
  • Local stability and global capture are different failure mechanisms; near the fold, an update can be locally stable yet still leave the tracking basin.
  • Learning-rate tuning only rescales the step, whereas reparameterization reshapes the kernel K and therefore the optimization geometry itself.

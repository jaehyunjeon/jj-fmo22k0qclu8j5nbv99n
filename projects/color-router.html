Inverse Design of Nanophotonic Color Router Robust to Oblique Incidence

Adv. Opt. Mater. 2026 · co-first author

KAIST · Quantum Nano Optics Lab

J. Jeon†, C. Park†, D. Heo, H. Chung, M. S. Jang, “Inverse design of nanophotonic color router robust to oblique incidence,” Advanced Optical Materials 14(4), e01697 (2026).

PaperKAIST news

Abstract

Nanophotonic color routers can replace absorptive color filters in CMOS image sensors, but designs optimized for normal incidence degrade severely when light arrives at an angle. Using large-scale adjoint-based inverse design across multiple incident angles and wavelengths, I designed a freeform color router that keeps about 78% optical efficiency within ±12°, and addressed inter-pixel crosstalk within the same framework.

Problem

A color router is a metamaterial that steers different wavelengths to different sub-pixels, collecting far more light than absorptive Bayer filters. Prior designs were optimized for a single, normal-incidence plane wave and nearly failed when the illumination was tilted by around 12°, as it is in real cameras.

Approach

  • Built a large-scale gradient-based inverse-design pipeline using the adjoint variable method and automatic differentiation, and identified the bottleneck that had prevented reproducing state-of-the-art designs.
  • Optimized the freeform structure jointly over multiple incident angles and wavelengths.
  • Treated inter-pixel crosstalk by representing a localized Gaussian beam as a coherent superposition of plane waves over a range of angles, so crosstalk and angular robustness are handled in one framework rather than as separate constraints.

Result

The optimized router keeps about 78% optical efficiency within a ±12° range of incidence, where earlier designs suffered critical degradation.

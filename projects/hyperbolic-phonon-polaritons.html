Selective Excitation of Higher-Order Hyperbolic Phonon-Polaritonic Modes

Paper in preparation · co-first author

KAIST · Quantum Nano Optics Lab

J. Jeon†, H. Kim†, M. Jang, S. Menabde, M. S. Jang, “Selective Excitation of Higher-Order Hyperbolic Phonon Polaritonic Mode,” in preparation.

Figure coming soon

Abstract

I develop an analytic mode-matching framework, based on the transfer-matrix method, that captures conversion among image-polariton, phonon-polariton, and radiation modes at metal discontinuities. Combined with an adjoint optimization framework I built on the COMSOL API, I inverse-design an Au grating beneath hBN that selectively excites a desired higher-order hyperbolic phonon-polariton mode, and am validating the design with SNOM experiments.

Problem

Hyperbolic phonon polaritons in hBN support a ladder of higher-order modes with extreme confinement, but far-field illumination couples mostly to the fundamental mode. The goal is to design an Au grating underneath hBN so that a chosen higher-order mode is selectively excited.

Analytic mode-matching framework

  • Built a transfer-matrix description in which each discontinuity converts between the eigenmodes on either side, using the unconjugated bilinear pairing appropriate for reciprocal, lossy systems.
  • Discretized the radiation and evanescent continua with PEC boundaries to include non-guided channels while keeping an orthogonal modal basis.
  • Analyzed the different projection formulations of the mode-conversion relation, which are equivalent only in the complete-basis limit, and compared their well-posedness under finite truncation. Introduced the full Gram matrix for non-orthogonal truncated bases.

Inverse design and experiment

  • Implemented adjoint-based shape optimization through the COMSOL API (adjoint source construction and gradient calculation), later generalized for use by other lab members.
  • Inverse-designed the grating geometry for selective higher-order excitation; experimental validation with scattering-type SNOM is in progress.

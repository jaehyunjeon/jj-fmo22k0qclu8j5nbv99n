Fundamental Limits of Tunable Nanophotonic Devices and Photonic Time Crystals

Ongoing research

KAIST · Quantum Nano Optics Lab

Figure coming soon

Abstract

I apply a Maxwell-constrained QCQP framework to bound the achievable performance of thermo-optically tunable metasurfaces with 3D diffraction-order control, asking whether inverse-designed devices are already near a limit set by materials and design space. I then extend the framework from binary spatial metasurfaces to binary photonic time crystals, deriving bounds on momentum-bandgap width and Floquet amplification rate under bounded modulation.

Motivation

Inverse design tells us how good a particular device can be made; fundamental-limit theory tells us how good any device could be. Bringing the two together changes the question from “how much further can this optimization go?” to “is the current design already close to what physics allows?”

Tunable metasurfaces

  • Formulated performance bounds for thermo-optically tunable metasurfaces with 3D diffraction-order control as a quadratically constrained quadratic program (QCQP) with Maxwell constraints.
  • Compared the bounds against inverse-designed freeform devices.

Photonic time crystals

  • Extended the framework from binary spatial structures to binary temporal modulation.
  • Derived bounds on the achievable momentum-bandgap width and Floquet amplification rate under bounded temporal modulation.

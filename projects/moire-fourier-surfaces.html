Analytical and Inverse Design Framework for Moiré Fourier Photonic Surfaces

Ongoing research

KAIST · Quantum Nano Optics Lab

Figure coming soon

Abstract

I develop a Floquet–Fourier framework for computing polaritonic band structures of conductivity-modulated Fourier surfaces and commensurate moiré superlattices. Making the band-structure model differentiable turns the search for a complete global bandgap into an eigenmode inverse-design problem, by mapping band-edge eigenstates to reciprocal-space scattering channels and optimizing symmetry-adapted harmonics for cavity formation.

Overview

Fourier surfaces, metal surfaces patterned with a few chosen spatial harmonics, offer a compact design space for shaping polaritonic bands of hBN placed on top. Finding a complete global bandgap for cavity design by sweeping geometries had become a bottleneck.

Approach

  • Derived and implemented a 2D Floquet–Fourier formulation for band structures of conductivity-modulated Fourier surfaces and commensurate moiré superlattices.
  • Recognized the problem as eigenmode optimization (design parameters modify the operator; eigenvalues and eigenmodes set the behavior), the same structure used in gradient-based design of tight-binding Hamiltonians.
  • Made the model differentiable and designed a Fourier-harmonic inverse-design strategy that maps band-edge eigenstates to reciprocal-space scattering channels and optimizes symmetry-adapted harmonics.

Result

Identified a Fourier surface with a complete global bandgap near the target frequency, opening a route to polaritonic cavity design.

Bound States in the Continuum in High-Dimensional Shape Space

Paper in preparation · co-first author

Stanford University · Fan Group · 2026 – present

J. Jeon†, T. Dai†, J. Fan, “Connected Manifolds of Bound States in the Continuum for Continuous Superchirality Control,” in preparation.

Abstract

I derive how the Q factor of a quasi-BIC scales along an arbitrary path through geometry space: the exponent is set by the order of contact between the path and the radiation-zero set, giving Q ∼ |δ|−2μ. Using this law as a search criterion, I locate symmetry-unprotected accidental BICs and show that exact BICs form connected constraint manifolds in high-dimensional shape space, which can be navigated to tune near-field superchirality while staying bound.

Generalized Q-scaling under arbitrary geometric perturbations

Instead of treating Q as the fundamental quantity, I describe radiation through the complex coupling amplitude D, with Q ∝ |D|−2. Expanding D along an arbitrary path in shape space shows that the Q-scaling exponent is set by the order of contact between the path and the radiation-zero set. A generic crossing gives the familiar Q ∼ |τ|−2; a path tangent to the zero set gives Q ∼ |τ|−4, and higher-order contact gives Q ∼ |τ|−2μ.

From scaling law to search criterion

Starting from randomly perturbed quasi-BIC geometries, I treated the existence of a nearby radiation zero as a hypothesis, followed directions that cancel first-order radiation, and searched for the predicted higher-order contact. This located symmetry-unprotected accidental BICs.

Connected BIC manifolds and superchirality

The BIC condition imposes only a few radiation constraints, so the null space of the radiation Jacobian leaves many geometric directions in which the structure can change without opening a radiative channel. I parameterize level-set geometries with SVD-derived geometric modes, compute shape gradients of a superchirality objective with eigenmode perturbation theory, and project them onto the tangent space of the BIC set. The goal is not another isolated high-Q design, but a navigable family of BICs whose remaining freedom tunes an independent near-field response.
